# Swipe File — 6 LPs that convert, annotated

Real landing pages that ship. Each entry: the page, the framework it uses, the load-bearing moves, and what you can steal. Read the one that matches your framework selection from `frameworks.md`.

Don't copy the copy. Copy the *structural moves.*

---

## 1. Basecamp — `basecamp.com`

**Framework:** PAS at Schwartz level 2 (problem-aware audience).

**Hero:**
> Basecamp Is the Project Management System That Helps Small Teams Move Faster and Work More Efficiently — Together.

**Why it works:**
- Names the *literal what* in the first 8 words ("project management system").
- Names the audience ("small teams") — filters out enterprise visitors and locks in fit.
- Names two outcomes back-to-back ("move faster," "work more efficiently").

**Load-bearing move:** the "since 2004" trust strip directly under the hero. Most pages put proof at the bottom. Basecamp puts it where the reader is still scanning.

**Steal this:** if you have any age advantage, surface it within the first scroll. New isn't automatically better.

## 2. Stripe — `stripe.com`

**Framework:** AIDA at Schwartz level 3 (solution-aware, picking between options).

**Hero:**
> Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue.

**Why it works:**
- Four words after the article. "Financial infrastructure" positions the category (not "payments tool," not "checkout API" — a whole layer).
- "Grow your revenue" is the *only* outcome that matters for the audience (CEOs/CFOs choosing payment stacks).
- Skips features entirely. The hero argues *category position*, not deliverables.

**Load-bearing move:** the customer logo wall as a section, not a strip. Each logo gets a context tile when you hover. The proof IS the product positioning.

**Steal this:** if your audience picks between you and 3 alternatives, your hero should argue *category position* before features. Features are the third scroll, not the first.

## 3. ConvertKit (Kit) — `kit.com`

**Framework:** StoryBrand at Schwartz level 3.

**Hero:**
> Email marketing for your creative business.

**Why it works:**
- Identity-first ("creative business") — the entire audience filter is baked into 4 words.
- "Email marketing" is the literal what.
- Subhead extends with the identity move: "Connect with your audience, grow your following, and earn a living doing what you love."

**Load-bearing move:** the testimonial block uses *named creators with revenue numbers.* Not "Sarah, content creator." Instead: "Pat Flynn, $300K+/year." Specificity makes the proof unforgeable.

**Steal this:** if you have any user with a public revenue or outcome number, name them and the number. One named creator with $300K/year beats 50 anonymous testimonials.

## 4. Linear — `linear.app`

**Framework:** AIDA at Schwartz level 3-4.

**Hero:**
> Linear is a purpose-built tool for planning and building products.

**Why it works:**
- "Purpose-built" is the positioning move — it implies the alternatives are general-purpose. Specifically a knock on Jira without naming Jira.
- "Planning and building products" names the job — not the deliverable, the job.

**Load-bearing move:** the entire scroll is animations of the product in motion. You see issues being created, kanbans flowing, keyboard shortcuts firing. The page IS the demo.

**Steal this:** if your product produces a visible output (a UI, an artifact, a result), put motion of the output above the fold. Static screenshots are 2010. Motion of the actual product converts.

## 5. Hormozi $100M Offers — `acquisition.com/training/100m-offers`

**Framework:** Hormozi Grand Slam Offer (literally — this is the source pattern).

**Hero:**
> How to Make Offers So Good People Feel Stupid Saying No

**Why it works:**
- Outcome promise that's specific and provocative.
- "Feel stupid saying no" is the *emotional after-state* of the buyer, named bluntly.
- Single sentence does the work of a paragraph. No subhead needed.

**Load-bearing move:** the value stack is shown as a literal *graphic* — $X + $X + $X = $Y, then "Your price: $Z." The math is presented as a visual proof, not buried in body copy.

**Steal this:** if you're stacking value, make the math a *visual element* on the page, not a paragraph. People skim. Math in a box gets seen; math in prose gets skipped.

## 6. heymitch — `heymitch.ai`

**Framework:** StoryBrand at Schwartz level 3 (identity + category position).

**Hero pattern:** "Your Agent Can [Verb] [Outcome]." (each skill page uses this same structure)

**Why it works:**
- Reframes the relationship — the visitor's agent is the actor, not the visitor. Identity move ("you are someone who has an agent that does things").
- "Can [verb]" is permission/capability framing — implies before they couldn't, now they can.
- Outcome is named in literal terms.

**Load-bearing moves:**
- Each free skill is its own LP at `heymitch-[skill].vercel.app`. Same shape, different content. Reusable factory.
- Floppy disk imagery on every page — a recurring visual metaphor that turns abstract software into a tangible product.
- Single inline email form. No "Submit." The button text is always "Get the Skill."
- The resources hub at `heymitch.ai/resources` collects every skill page into a grid. Each LP funnels into the hub; the hub funnels into more skills.

**Steal this:** if you have multiple free products / lead magnets, give each one a dedicated LP and a *physical metaphor* (floppy disk, vinyl record, ticket, scene card — whatever fits your brand). Abstract software fights for attention. Tangible objects don't.

## What every entry shares

If you only remember 5 moves from this file:

1. **Name the literal what in the hero.** "Email marketing for X" beats "elevate your customer experience."
2. **Audience filter in the headline or subhead.** Your hero should make wrong-fit visitors bounce. Right-fit visitors thank you for the filter.
3. **One named proof beats many vague proofs.** "Sarah shipped 12 deals" > "47 happy customers."
4. **Motion or visual math above the fold when possible.** Static screenshots have lost; product motion or value-stack visuals carry the page.
5. **CTA is verb + outcome.** "Get the skill" / "Book the call" / "Buy the course." Never "Submit" or "Get started."

If a section of your draft doesn't follow these moves, ask what it's doing instead. If the answer is "looking professional," kill it and try one of these moves.
