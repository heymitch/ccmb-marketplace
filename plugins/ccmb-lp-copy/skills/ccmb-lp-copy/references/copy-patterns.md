# Copy Patterns — Headlines, Heroes, CTAs, Sections

Formulas for each major section. Pick by framework + audience awareness level (see `frameworks.md` §5). Never write from a blank page — start with a pattern, then break it on purpose.

---

## 1. Headlines — 25 patterns that work

### Outcome-first (works for AIDA, Hormozi)

1. `[Outcome] for [specific role]` — "Async deals for solo consultants."
2. `[Outcome] in [timeframe], without [common cost]` — "Ship your AI product in 4 weeks, without writing code."
3. `From [before-state] to [after-state] in [time]` — "From idea to live product in 14 days."
4. `[Verb] [outcome] without [pain]` — "Sell digital products without paying for ads."
5. `The [outcome] system for [audience]` — "The recurring-revenue system for solo consultants."

### Identity-first (works for StoryBrand)

6. `Become the [identity] who [achieves outcome]` — "Become the consultant who ships products on the side."
7. `For [identity] ready to [transformation]` — "For writers ready to stop trading hours for dollars."
8. `You're a [role]. You want [outcome]. Here's how.` — "You're a designer. You want to ship a real product. Here's how."
9. `The [adjective] [identity] guide to [outcome]` — "The reluctant founder's guide to shipping."

### Problem-first (works for PAS, Schwartz level 2)

10. `Stop [losing X] every time you [try Y]` — "Stop losing deals every time you write a proposal."
11. `Your [thing] keeps [bad pattern]. Here's why.` — "Your launches keep flopping. Here's why."
12. `Why [common method] fails — and what works instead` — "Why cold outreach fails — and what works instead."
13. `[Specific problem] — solved` — "Stale onboarding emails — solved."

### Curiosity / contrarian (works for cold traffic, content marketing)

14. `Most [audience] are [doing X]. The 5% who [do Y] are [outcome].` — "Most consultants charge by the hour. The 5% who productize are 4x more profitable."
15. `What [trusted source] does that [common practice] doesn't` — "What Stripe does that most checkout pages don't."
16. `The [unexpected thing] that [outcome]` — "The one-page brief that closes 90% of consulting deals."
17. `Why I stopped [common practice] (and you should too)` — "Why I stopped using Calendly (and switched to async deals)."

### Mechanism-led (works when you have a novel approach)

18. `The [named mechanism] for [outcome]` — "The skill stack method for shipping AI products."
19. `[Outcome] using [unique mechanism]` — "Recurring revenue using async deal pages."
20. `Introducing [named mechanism]: [outcome]` — "Introducing the codify loop: ship once, every future site is born correct."

### Specificity / number-led (works for case studies, results pages)

21. `How [specific person] [outcome] in [timeframe]` — "How Sarah shipped 12 deals in 30 days."
22. `[N] [audience] [outcome]. Here's the system.` — "47 founders shipped their first paid product in 6 weeks. Here's the system."
23. `From $0 to $[X] in [time] (the [mechanism])` — "From $0 to $10K MRR in 90 days (the async deal method)."

### Direct command (works for Schwartz level 4-5, warm audiences)

24. `[Get / Buy / Join / Start] [thing]` — "Join the next cohort."
25. `[Action] before [deadline]` — "Lock in $297 before Friday."

### Auto-reject — clichés that get killed

- "Revolutionize / transform / unleash / supercharge your [anything]"
- "The ultimate guide to [anything]"
- "Unlock the secret to [anything]"
- "Take your [thing] to the next level"
- "Are you tired of [pain]?" (banned PAS shortcut)
- "Imagine if you could [outcome]" (subjunctive — concrete only)
- "[Brand] is the leading [category] platform" (brand-as-hero — kill)

If your headline matches any of these, reroll.

## 2. Subheads — the "what + how fast" template

After the headline, the subhead does two jobs:

1. **Clarify** what the product *literally is* (a course, a tool, a service, a download).
2. **Specify** the timeframe or activation speed.

### Templates

- `[What it is] — [outcome] in [time].`
- `[What it does], all through [familiar mechanism]. [Activation time].`
- `[Concrete deliverable] for [audience]. [Time / format].`

### Examples

- "A 6-week cohort that ships your first paid AI product. Starts March 18."
- "Free skill install for Claude Code. Setup in 2 minutes."
- "Async deal pages that close $5K+ consulting engagements without calls. One-time $497."

### Cap rules

- One sentence. Period. (Two short sentences if absolutely needed, but try one first.)
- Under 20 words.
- Concrete deliverable named. No "comprehensive solution for your needs."

## 3. Benefits sections — outcome + mechanism, never features

### The pattern

Each benefit = `[Outcome the reader cares about]` + `[Brief mechanism that makes it real]`.

NOT: "Built-in analytics dashboard"  (feature)
YES: "See exactly which page converts — analytics built into every deploy"  (outcome + mechanism)

NOT: "AI-powered content generation"  (feature)
YES: "Ship a week of content in an hour — your CLAUDE.md becomes the spec"  (outcome + mechanism)

### Section structure

- **3-5 items.** Not 7. Not 10. Three is best.
- Headline above the grid: "What you get" / "How it works" / "Why it's different" / framework-specific.
- Each item:
  - **Title:** the outcome, 3-6 words.
  - **Body:** one sentence. Mechanism + specificity.

### Example block

> ## What you get
>
> **Recurring deals on autopilot.**  
> Each engagement is async — page goes live, prospect reads, decides, signs, pays. No call required.
>
> **Closed loops, never half-finished.**  
> Each page includes the brief, scope, payment terms, and onboarding next step. They can't sign without committing to the whole arc.
>
> **One template, infinite forks.**  
> Edit the brief, redeploy. Same shape, different deal. Build once, run dozens.

### Red flags

- Three benefits that all say the same thing in different words. Cut to two.
- Benefits that are actually features ("dashboard," "API access," "templates"). Rewrite as outcomes.
- "Save time" as a benefit. Everything saves time. Be specific — "Save 6 hours/week on proposals."

## 4. CTAs — verb + outcome, repeated

### Button text patterns

| Context | Good | Banned |
|---|---|---|
| Free skill / download | "Get the Skill" / "Download the Guide" | "Submit" / "Click here" / "Download" (verb alone) |
| Free trial | "Start free trial" / "Try it free" | "Sign up" / "Get started" |
| Paid course | "Enroll in Cohort 3" / "Buy the course" | "Purchase" / "Buy now" (no specificity) |
| Consulting / service | "Book a 15-min call" / "Apply for the program" | "Contact us" / "Learn more" |
| Email opt-in (lead magnet) | "Get the Skill" / "Send me the guide" | "Subscribe" / "Join" |

### The "verb + outcome" rule

Always start with a verb. Always end with what they get. The middle clarifies if needed.

- ❌ "Submit"
- ❌ "Sign up"
- ✅ "Get the cohort details"
- ✅ "Send me Week 1"
- ✅ "Book your strategy call"

### Repeat the CTA

- Lead-magnet: once, in the form.
- Full page: 2-3 times throughout the scroll. Hero CTA + mid-page form + bottom-page form. Same text, same button color.
- Hormozi long-form: 4+ times. After hero, after value stack, after risk reversal, in the FAQ section, at the bottom.

### Form CTA + headline

Always pair the form with a short headline:
- "Enter your email to get the [thing]"
- "Reserve your seat for Cohort 3"
- "Get the brief"

And a microcopy below:
- "Free download. No spam."
- "We email you the cohort details + dates."
- "Reply to confirm. We don't share your address."

The microcopy reduces form anxiety. Always include it.

## 5. Social proof sections

### Three forms that work

1. **Single named case study with metric.** "Sarah, a freelance designer, shipped 12 deals in 30 days using this system. Average deal size: $4,200." Beats 10 vague testimonials.
2. **Metric block.** "47 founders shipped. $1.2M in deal volume. 6-week median time to first deal." Numbers > adjectives.
3. **Logo wall with one-line context.** Each logo + "[Team] at [logo] uses this to [specific job]." Logos alone are dead air.

### Patterns that don't work

- 5 anonymous testimonials with "John D." style attribution. Reader assumes fabricated.
- "Trusted by 10,000+ users" with no proof. Round numbers feel made up.
- Star ratings with no source. Where did the stars come from?

### If you have no social proof yet

**Drop the section.** Don't fabricate. Pages without social proof can still convert if the framework is strong (PAS) and the offer is specific (Hormozi-style price/value math). Faking proof kills trust permanently.

Use the empty space for an extra benefits item or a longer FAQ. Fill the section gap structurally, not emotionally.

## 6. FAQ sections — objection map, not curiosity satisfier

### Pattern

5-7 questions. Each question maps to an objection — *what the reader is silently worried about but won't ask.*

### The objection map for most LPs

| Objection | FAQ question |
|---|---|
| "This won't work for someone like me" | "Does this work for [specific edge case in audience]?" |
| "I don't have time" | "How much time does this actually take per week?" |
| "I've tried things like this and they didn't work" | "How is this different from [common alternative]?" |
| "I'm not technical enough" | "Do I need to know [scary thing]?" |
| "What if it doesn't work?" | "What if I don't get [outcome]?" → risk reversal answer |
| "Can I afford this?" | "Is this worth [price]?" → value math |
| "How fast will I see results?" | "How long until I [first outcome milestone]?" |

### Format

- Q: short question (under 15 words), in the reader's voice.
- A: 2-3 sentences. Direct. Don't dodge the objection — name it and answer it.
- No headlines inside answers. No bulleted lists. Conversational.

### Skip

- Questions you wish were objections but aren't. ("What inspired this product?" → kill, reader doesn't care.)
- Questions where the honest answer hurts your offer. Either fix the offer or remove the question.
- More than 8 questions. Long FAQs signal a confused offer. Tighten the page first.

## 7. Hero layouts — 3 shapes that fit most products

### Shape A: Lead-magnet (single column)

```
[Headline, left-aligned, large]
[Subheadline below, smaller, max-width ~50ch]
[Email input — Button]  (single row on desktop, stacked on mobile)
[Microcopy: "Free download. No spam."]
```

For: free skill, free guide, email opt-in.

### Shape B: Course / cohort (two column)

```
[Headline + Subheadline + CTA button]  |  [Product image / cover / floppy disk]
                                       |
[Section break — first proof or benefit immediately below]
```

For: paid product where there's a *thing to look at* (course cover, product mockup).

### Shape C: Service / consulting (single column + frame)

```
[Eyebrow text: "For [specific role]"]
[Headline, centered, large]
[Subheadline below]
[CTA button: "Book a 15-min call"]
[Below: trust strip — 3 logos or "Used by [N] [audience]"]
```

For: high-touch services, consulting, agency pitches. No product image — the deliverable is *you.*

### Picking

- Lead magnet → Shape A.
- Course / cohort / digital product → Shape B.
- Service / consulting → Shape C.
- Anything else → start with Shape A and add complexity if needed. Reader can't bounce on a hero they can read in 4 seconds.
