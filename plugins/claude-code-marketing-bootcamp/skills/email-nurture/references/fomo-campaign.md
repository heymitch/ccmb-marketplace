## Module #1: Email Marketing 101

Do any of these sound familiar?

“I don’t want to sound salesy!”
“If I pitch my audience people will unsubscribe!”
“Email marketing is way too pushy—I’ll never do it!”

Unfortunately, this is a feeling among most creators, solopreneurs, and business owners who sell digital products.

Whether it’s eBooks, courses, templates, or services, writers worry that directly pitching (or “squeezing”) their list will come across as salesy or pushy. They fear their audience will resent it, leading to a mass exodus of subscribers.

We’re here to tell you that this mindset is fundamentally flawed.

Email marketing is one of the main drivers of sales in our flagship cohort-based course Ship 30 for 30.

It’s how we’ve been able to teach over 10,000 students the fundamentals of Digital Writing. The course has helped people go onto make a living for themselves as a writer, transformed their lives beyond just writing, and reimagine what’s possible for themselves. At every celebration call we have someone tell us they were unsure whether they should take the plunge, but are so grateful they did.

So if you truly believe your digital product or offer can provide immense value and transformation for your audience, you have an obligation to do everything you can to get them to buy.

This is the actual reality of email marketing, and with a little bit of thought, you don’t have to come across as “salesy”.

Still not convinced? Here are some ways of thinking about email marketing (and why you shouldn’t be afraid of “rejection”):

- You worked hard to provide free value (with your social content and newsletter)—now give people a reason to take the next step and invest in your product or service.
- If someone doesn't enroll, they should keep getting value from your emails, not be ignored until the next launch (keep sending them your weekly newsletter!)
- Don't be afraid of unsubscribes—having a smaller list of truly engaged subscribers is better than a big, disengaged list. Focus on serving your ideal customers.
- Your goal should be to help as many people as possible achieve the results your product promises.

So how do you sell a product or service to your list without sounding “pushy?”

It’s simple: You help people rationalize the decision through email education and framing the immense value being offered.

Because if you truly believe your program is transformative, then educating them on those outcomes is easy (and not at all “sleazy”, “salesy”, or “pushy”).

Now, let’s dive into the 7 “FOMO” emails you need to know how to write in order to sell ANY product or service—along with templates for each (so you can get to work!).

## Module #2: Our 7 “FOMO” Email Campaign

Seven days before the start of every Ship 30 cohort, we send these 7 emails to encourage people to enroll in the course.

(For context: These 7 emails have generated over $3,000,000 in revenue for Ship 30 for 30.)

By this point, the people on our list have received a ton of free value from us:

- They follow us on social media or have seen us on a podcast
- They’ve been through one of our Educational Email Courses which give them everything they need to get started
- Every week we send them a newsletter jam-packed with actionable tips, strategies, and lessons to help them start writing online

So, the next step is to be direct and pitch them on enrolling into Ship 30. But we do this in a way that isn’t salesy. Instead, we educate them on the value provided in the course, the transformations they can expect, and the opportunity cost of not taking action right now.

### Email 1 - The Transformation

We frame this first email by asking the reader: “Where would you be if you started a year ago?”

This taps into the human tendency of putting off changes they want to make. The mental flip is effective because:

- Reason 1: It helps readers imagine the future benefits, priming them to want those outcomes.
- Reason 2: It creates a vision for where readers could be in their lives/careers in a year if they start now.
- Reason 3: It frames the decision as "start now or waste another year stuck", creating a sense of urgency.

If you have customers who’ve gone onto be successful because of your product, mention them here (a short quote or testimonial is ideal). When you first start out, you won’t be able to point to successful people who’ve purchased your products, but you can still use this to “future pace” the outcomes for the reader.

Example: The Tale Of 2 Young Men (Wall Street Journal Sales Letter)

### Email 2 - The Financial Outcomes

The higher the price point of your product, the more you need to point to a financial ROI.

College costs can spiral beyond $150,000. But it costs a lot because (usually) you get paid more in a job you couldn’t get without a college degree. Customers use this same logic when evaluating your product.

You need to give evidence (in the form of a case study) that it has some kind of positive financial ROI if your reader buys. It helps the prospective customer see the possible financial outcomes, and helps them mentally justify the investment.

### Email 3 - The Price Anchor

What’s the easiest way to sell a $5,000 watch? You put it next to a $50,000 watch.

If your potential customer is going to buy, it’s important to educate them on why you’re charging what you’re charging and why it’s such a good deal. In this email:

- Breakdown your product by listing out everything they’ll be getting
- Give the reader the value of each item
- Then drop the price you’re actually charging (with any discounts you’re offering)

Your reader’s takeaway: this is an absurd amount of value at a laughably low price.

### Email 4 - The Challenges and The Benefits

Split into 2 parts:

- **The Challenges**: List out all the challenges your target audience is facing or feeling. Framing it as “we know you’re facing [challenge]” shows empathy and that you have the solution.
- **The Benefits**: Take all the challenges and explain how your product solves every single one of them and the benefit of solving it. Link each challenge to your solution and the outcomes it unlocks.

Pro copywriting tip: if you’re using an onboarding survey and asking what challenges your customer is facing, you can literally copy paste the exact language being used into this email. One customer’s experience is shared by hundreds of others, and the language is a copywriting goldmine. You will literally be saying back to them what they’re already thinking.

### Email 5 - The Success Stories

This email is designed to create FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out):

“Here’s someone who bought the product and is now crushing it.” “Here’s another person unlocking all the outcomes you want for yourself.” “Here’s another person. And another person. And another person.”

When people see others succeeding (and doing what they want to do), it makes them want the same results. Show how people who take action with your product get all the outcomes and benefits you spoke about in the previous email. Add as many case studies and success stories as you can. Use storytelling: describe where your success story was before they used your product, how your product helped them overcome the challenge, and the massive outcomes they’re seeing now. Then a short testimonial quote.

### Email 6 - The In-Depth Case Study

Works like Email 5, but instead of listing tons of successful customers, go deep with just one of your most successful customers.

- What challenges did this customer face before they bought your product?
- What measurable outcomes have they unlocked since then?
- How would they describe their transformation (in their own words)?

You’re adding an extra layer of credibility and social proof AND future pacing the possible outcomes.

### Email 7 - The Last Chance

People are busy. They might miss an email or not read the landing page properly. So in your final email:

- Give them a full rundown of everything they get when they buy your product.
- List more testimonials and social proof from successful customers and/or alumni.
- Tell them this is their FINAL chance to buy.
- If you’re selling an evergreen product, create urgency by explaining the opportunity cost of not taking action now.

## Module #4: How To Write Irresistible Subject Lines

The 7 “FOMO” emails are all you need to pitch your list. But an important sub-skill is writing Irresistible Subject Lines—so people open them.

By far, the biggest mistake writers make is making subject lines “too long.” If a subject line gets cut off before “the hook” is revealed, the chances of a reader clicking is very low.

### Rule #1: 5-15 words, max.

A subject line should not exceed 15 words. Ever. Less is always more. If you can say it in 15 words, try 10. If 10, try 7. If 7, try 5. But "less words" doesn't inherently mean better — the key is to give the reader a “hook” that makes them want to click, in as few words as possible WITHOUT LOSING THE MEANING.

Example: “The 1 thing you need to do in order to strengthen the microbiome in your gut.” (16 words). Shortened badly: “The 1 thing you need to do” (loses the hook). Better rewrite: “Want to strengthen your gut microbiome? Try this 1 thing.” (10 words, same idea).

### Rule #2: Subject lines should read like you're talking to a friend.

This comes down to grammar & punctuation. Compare:

- “3 Mistakes Keeping You From Landing Your First $5,000”
- “3 mistakes keeping you from landing your first $5,000…”

The second sees better open rates because it’s written in sentence-case (feels like a friend emailing a friend) versus Capitalized (reads like an article headline). Small thing, but a good nuance.

### Rule #3: Use 1 of the 4 proven hooks.

Four hooks work over and over regardless of industry, niche, or topic:

1. "Here's a ton of value for minimal time." — *Your entire Life Coach career path blueprint… in 1 email!*
2. "Here's a ton of value for minimal cost." — *Here's our entire $1M marketing plan. Steal it!*
3. "Here's how to solve your problem without much effort." — *Losing money in the stock market? Just buy these 3 companies.*
4. "Here's how to unlock this desirable outcome, instantly." — *3 keys to land $10,000 consulting clients, from home, in your pajamas, right now.*

Always aim to use 1 of these 4. Otherwise the reader won't have a good enough reason to click.

### Rule #4: Use visceral language!

Amp up subject lines with visceral language: OUCH! · URGENT! · FREE! · Seriously… · Steal this! · (I can't believe I'm giving this away.)

- (Original) Losing money in the stock market? Just buy these 3 companies.
- (Amped Up) OUCH! Losing money in the stock market? Just buy these 3 companies.
- (Original) Here's our entire $1M marketing plan. Steal it!
- (Amped Up) [FOR FREE!] Here's our entire $1M marketing plan. Steal it!

Small thing, but can increase open rates by a few percentage points.

### Rule #5: Name & Claim ideas (in the subject line)

The ultimate goal is to Name & Claim ideas right in the subject line:

- (V1) 3 ways to make more money as a life coach
- (V2, Amped Up) 3 easy ways to make 10x more money as a life coach (nobody tells you this).
- (V3, Named & Claimed) Life Coach Money Manual: 3 easy ways to 10x your monthly earnings (nobody tells you this)

“Life Coach Money Manual” feels like you're receiving a “thing,” which makes it feel exponentially more valuable. The challenge is doing this WITHOUT exceeding the word count limit. V3 is 16 words, so trim:

- (15 words) Life Coach Money Manual: 3 ways to 10x your monthly earnings (nobody tells you this)
- (14 words) Life Coach Money Manual: How to 10x your monthly earnings (nobody tells you this)
- (13 words) Life Coach Money Manual: 3 (little known) ways to 10x your monthly earnings

Start by writing the most effective subject line you can (based on 1 of the 4 hooks), THEN worry about trimming it down (without losing the essence) last.
